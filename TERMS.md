# Merged Cells level terms

Last changed: 22 November 2026

These terms cover the level library in every build of Merged Cells, and levels you send the developer for the game (see "Sending a level for the game"). They're separate from the [privacy policy](PRIVACY.md), which says what data the game keeps and for how long.

## What the library is

The level library is where players share the levels they make in Merged Cells. You can play other players' levels, like them, and publish your own. Joining the library ("Join the level library?" in the game, or "Use the level library" in Settings → Privacy and data) means you accept these terms. If you don't, the library stays hidden and you can still make and play your own levels, and share them as codes.

The game is for people aged 13 and over. If you're under 16, check with a parent before you join.

## What you can publish

You can publish levels you made yourself. Levels you got from someone else can't be published, and nor can a level whose layout is already in the library or is one of the game's own levels. If you start from a copy, change it before you publish it.

A published level, and its name, can be seen by everyone who uses the library.

## Your levels and what publishing allows

You still own the levels you make. Publishing one doesn't take it away from you: you can keep playing it, changing it and sharing it, in the game and anywhere else, and take it out of the library whenever you like.

By publishing a level, you give two permissions:

- **Other players** can play it, share it and make their own copies of it in Merged Cells, and publish a copy once they've changed it.
- **The developer** can use the level and its name in any way they like, anywhere, without paying you or naming you as its maker. That includes copying, changing, publishing and selling it, putting it in the game (as one of the game's own levels, for example), using it in screenshots, videos and adverts, and letting anyone else do any of these.

Both permissions are free, worldwide and permanent, and you can't take them back. Removing a level or deleting your data takes it out of the library and off the server, but doesn't undo anything already done with it, or stop the developer using a copy they already have. As far as the law allows, you also give up any right to be named as a level's maker or to object to how it's changed.

You can only give these permissions for a level that's yours, which is why you can only publish levels you made yourself.

## The game's own levels

The game's own levels belong to the developer, except those players sent in, which the developer uses with their makers' permission (see "Sending a level for the game"). The game lets you copy one you've earned three stars on, change and share your copy, and publish it once you've changed it. That doesn't make the original yours.

## Sending a level for the game

You can offer the developer a level you made to be one of the game's own levels, in the campaign for example. You don't need to join the library to do it. Send its share code to the developer, by email to definitely.not.zac@proton.me or however you usually reach them, and in the same message:

- say that you agree to this section of these terms
- say what name you'd like to be credited under, or that you'd rather not be credited

Sending a level gives the developer the same permission over it as publishing one (see "Your levels and what publishing allows"), and it's just as permanent, except that the developer credits you as below. You still own the level and can keep sharing it. The developer doesn't have to use it, and may change it before they do.

If your level, or one based on it, goes in the game, you're credited in the game's credits under the name you chose, unless you said you'd rather not be. Pick a name you're happy for anyone to see: a nickname is fine. To change the name, or to be left out of the credits, email the developer and the next update does it.

You can only send a level you made yourself. If you're under 16, check with a parent before you send one.

## What isn't allowed

Don't publish a level whose name or picture (blocks, walls and traps can draw shapes and words) is, or includes:

- hateful or harassing: attacking anyone for who they are, or bullying or threatening anyone
- sexual
- violent or threatening, or encouraging anyone to harm themselves or others
- anyone's personal details, such as a name, address, phone number or social media account
- illegal, or encouraging anything illegal
- spam, advertising, or pretending to be someone else, including the developer

## Reporting and blocking

Every level in the library has Report and "Block this player" on its sheet.

- **Report** sends the level and the reason you chose to the developer. A level that several players report is hidden from everyone until the developer has checked it.
- **Block this player** hides every level by whoever published that one, for you only. They aren't told. "Unblock everyone" at the end of the library undoes it.

## What happens to levels that break these terms

The developer checks reported levels and either puts them back or removes them for good. A removed level can't be published again under the same ID. A player who keeps breaking these terms can be stopped from publishing. Levels can also be removed without a report if they break these terms.

## If you disagree with a decision

If you think a level was removed, or you were stopped from publishing, by mistake, email definitely.not.zac@proton.me with the level's share code and why. The developer will look at it again and tell you the outcome. Complaints about how reports are handled go to the same address.

## Taking your levels out

"Remove from library" on your level's sheet takes it out of the library. "Delete my data" (Settings → Privacy and data) removes every level you published, with everything else the game has sent under your random ID. Copies other players already got stay on their phones, and neither takes back the permissions in "Your levels and what publishing allows".

## No promises

The library is offered as it is, for free, and may change, stop working for a while or end. The developer isn't responsible for levels other players publish, but will act on reports. Nothing in these terms limits your rights as a consumer, or any responsibility the law doesn't allow to be limited.

## Which law applies

These terms are under the law of England and Wales. If you live in Scotland or Northern Ireland, you can also bring a claim in your own courts. If you live somewhere else, you keep any protections your own country's law gives you.

## Changes

If these terms change, the game asks you again before you can use the library. The date at the top shows when they last changed.

## Contact
